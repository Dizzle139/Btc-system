Step 3: what was on the chart at the turns

Every turning point on daily closes was checked against the same list, using only what was known at that day's close. Each item is then compared with the days that looked the same on the surface: other days where price closed at a new 10-day low (for bottoms) or high (for tops) and did not turn. That is the question a trader faces live: price is making a new low, is this the low?

How a move is marked

  1. Track the highest high (up move) or lowest low (down move) since the last turn.
  2. When price reverses by the move size from that extreme, the extreme becomes a turning point and a new move starts there.
  3. A candle that sets a new extreme can't also confirm a reversal on the same day.
  4. Closes version: the same, using daily closes instead of highs and lows.
  5. Run at 10%, 20%, 30% and 50%. Each smaller move is tagged with the bigger move whose span contains its midpoint.

Checked against the raw candles: every confirmed move meets its size, moves alternate up and down with no gaps, and every turning point is the true extreme of its move, except the flagged cases.

What you're checking

  • Do the marked turning points match the tops and bottoms you'd mark yourself?
  • Wicks or closes: which one marks the moves you'd call real?
  • Which moves look like noise to you? Note them by date and I'll measure what they have in common.
  • Does the "size ÷ normal daily range" filter separate signal from noise the way you see it?
